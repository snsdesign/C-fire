Types of fires and extinguishers explained

Not every fire requires the same solution

Depending on the type of fire, a different extinguishing agent is required. Choosing the right device is essential to respond quickly, safely and efficiently. Below we explain the main fire classes and show which fire extinguisher is most suitable.

A

Fire class A

Fires involving solid materials such as wood, paper, textiles and cardboard.

Examples: furniture, archives, cardboard packaging.

Recommended:

  • Powder extinguishers
  • Foam extinguishers
  • Control Fire
B

Fire class B

Fires involving liquids or substances that can become liquid.

Examples: petrol, oil, paint, solvents.

Recommended:

  • CO₂ extinguishers
  • Powder extinguishers
  • Foam extinguishers
  • Control Fire
C

Fire class C

Fires involving gases.

Examples: propane, butane, natural gas.

Recommended:

  • Powder extinguishers
D

Fire class D

Fires involving metals.

Examples: magnesium, aluminium, sodium.

Recommended:

  • Control Fire
E

Fire class E

Fires where electricity is present. These are not separate fuels, but situations where electrical current poses a risk.

Examples: electrical installations, switch cabinets, machines under voltage.

Recommended:

  • Powder extinguishers
  • Foam extinguishers
  • Control Fire
F

Fire class F

Fires involving oils and fats at high temperatures.

Examples: deep fryers, cooking oil, kitchen fires.

Recommended:

  • Foam extinguishers
  • Control Fire

Important to remember

  • Always choose an extinguisher that fits the environment and risk.
  • Not every extinguishing agent is suitable for every type of fire.
  • Have fire extinguishers checked and serviced annually.
